Section 1

Summary of findings

The 2026 season ran from 2 to 15 July. A four-person team crossed the upper Kestrel basin on skis, hauling sledges between six survey stations set on the 2019 grid. At each station we drilled a shallow core, dug a snow pit and ran a ground-penetrating radar transect across the flow line.

The headline result is the thinning at the lower stations. Station K4, at the foot of the icefall, has lost 18 m of ice thickness since the 2019 survey, close to three times the loss measured at the plateau stations. Snow depth was near the long-term average everywhere except K5, where a late June storm left a wind-packed slab more than two metres deep.

Surface melt started early. The automatic weather station at K3 logged positive degree-days from the first morning, and the melt curve in Figure 1 shows the warm spell of 8 to 10 July clearly. Melt on those three days exceeded the whole of the first week.

All cores, pit profiles and radar files are archived under report KIS-2026-07. Station K6 could not be reached on the return leg because of crevassing, so its readings come from the outbound visit only. Recommendations for the 2027 season are on the sign-off page.
